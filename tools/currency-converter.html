Why your bank's rate is different

The rate shown is a mid-market reference: the midpoint between buying and selling prices in the wholesale market. Banks and payment providers add a spread on top, and some charge a fixed transfer fee as well. On a large supplier payment, a spread of even 1–3% can wipe out a meaningful part of your margin, so compare providers before you pay.

Managing currency risk on import orders

  • Price in your own currency where suppliers accept it — the supplier then carries the risk (and usually prices it in).
  • Lock in a rate with a forward contract if the gap between order and payment is long and the amount is large.
  • Match deposits and balances — budget the balance payment at a slightly worse rate than today.
  • Build a buffer of a few percent into landed cost when the currency pair is volatile.

Frequently asked questions

Where do these exchange rates come from?

From a free public reference feed (open.er-api.com by ExchangeRate-API) that updates daily. Rates are indicative and may lag fast-moving markets.

Should I pay suppliers in USD or their local currency?

Many Asian suppliers quote in USD. Paying in the supplier's local currency can sometimes earn a better price, but you then carry the exchange risk. Ask for quotes in both and compare.

Does this tool store my amounts?

No. Conversions run in your browser; only the public rate table is downloaded.
