The two families of rules

Any mode of transport (7): EXW, FCA, CPT, CIP, DAP, DPU and DDP. Use these for containers, air, road, rail and multimodal moves. Sea and inland waterway only (4): FAS, FOB, CFR and CIF — designed for goods loaded directly onto a vessel, such as bulk commodities.

Five rules that cause the most disputes

  • FOB for containers. Containers are handed over at a terminal days before loading, yet FOB moves risk only when goods are on board. FCA closes that gap.
  • EXW for exports. The buyer becomes responsible for export clearance in a country where they may have no legal presence.
  • CIF/CFR risk. The seller pays freight, but risk passes at the origin port — buyers often assume otherwise.
  • DDP for foreign sellers. The seller must act as importer and pay destination VAT, which is often impractical.
  • A vague named place. “FCA China” is not enough; name the exact address, terminal or port.
Always name the place and the version

Write the rule, a precise named place and the edition, e.g. “FCA Ningbo CFS, No. 88 Example Road, Incoterms® 2020”.

Frequently asked questions

Do Incoterms decide when ownership transfers?

No. Incoterms® rules cover delivery, risk, costs and customs duties. Transfer of title and payment are governed by your sales contract and applicable law.

What changed in Incoterms® 2020?

DAT became DPU, CIP now requires a higher (all-risks) level of insurance, FCA allows an on-board bill of lading if agreed, and parties may use their own transport under FCA, DAP, DPU and DDP.

Can I still use Incoterms 2010?

Yes, if the contract clearly says so — older editions remain usable by agreement. But most traders now specify the 2020 edition.
