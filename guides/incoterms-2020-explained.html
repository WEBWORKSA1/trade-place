Shipping terms

Incoterms® 2020 Explained: EXW, FCA, FOB, CIF, DDP and the Rest

All 11 Incoterms® 2020 rules explained in plain English, with a summary table of risk transfer, freight and insurance, the 2020 changes and common mistakes.

6 min read Updated October 2026 Trade.Place Editorial Team
Key takeaways
  • There are 11 Incoterms® 2020 rules: seven for any mode of transport and four only for sea and inland waterway.
  • Each rule defines where delivery and risk pass from seller to buyer, and who pays which costs. It does not decide when ownership transfers.
  • For containerised cargo, FCA, CPT and CIP usually fit better than FOB, CFR and CIF.
  • Always write the rule with a precise named place and the version, for example “FCA Seller’s Warehouse, Ningbo, Incoterms® 2020”.

Incoterms® rules are three-letter trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC). They give buyers and sellers a shared shorthand for who arranges transport, who pays for each part of the journey, who handles export and import clearance, and at what point the risk of loss or damage moves from seller to buyer. Incoterms® is a registered trademark of the ICC, and the official text is available from the ICC at iccwbo.org. This guide is a practical summary; for contracts, rely on the official publication.

What Incoterms do and do not cover

Incoterms cover the delivery obligations between seller and buyer: delivery point, transfer of risk, allocation of costs, export and import clearance, transport and insurance contracts, and the related documents. They do not cover the price, payment terms, when legal ownership passes, what happens if the contract is breached, or the terms of your contract with the carrier. Those belong in your sales contract.

The two groups of rules

Rules for any mode of transport

  • EXW – Ex Works: the seller makes goods available at its premises. The buyer does almost everything else, including loading and, in principle, export clearance.
  • FCA – Free Carrier: the seller clears goods for export and delivers them to the buyer’s carrier at a named place. If that place is the seller’s premises, the seller loads the goods.
  • CPT – Carriage Paid To: the seller pays transport to the named destination, but risk passes when goods are handed to the first carrier.
  • CIP – Carriage and Insurance Paid To: as CPT, plus the seller buys cargo insurance for the buyer’s benefit.
  • DAP – Delivered at Place: the seller delivers to the named destination, ready for unloading. The buyer handles import clearance and duties.
  • DPU – Delivered at Place Unloaded: as DAP, but the seller also unloads the goods at destination.
  • DDP – Delivered Duty Paid: the seller delivers to the destination, cleared for import with duties and taxes paid.

Rules for sea and inland waterway only

  • FAS – Free Alongside Ship: the seller places goods alongside the vessel at the named port of shipment.
  • FOB – Free on Board: the seller loads goods on board the vessel nominated by the buyer.
  • CFR – Cost and Freight: the seller pays sea freight to the destination port; risk passes once goods are on board at origin.
  • CIF – Cost, Insurance and Freight: as CFR, plus the seller buys minimum cargo insurance for the buyer.

The C-rules split cost and risk

Under CPT, CIP, CFR and CIF the seller pays freight to the destination, but risk passes at origin. If the goods are damaged at sea, it is the buyer who must claim, which is why insurance terms under these rules matter so much.

What changed in Incoterms 2020

  • DAT became DPU. The old Delivered at Terminal rule was renamed Delivered at Place Unloaded, making clear that the unloading place does not have to be a terminal.
  • Higher insurance under CIP. CIP now requires broader cover, in line with Institute Cargo Clauses (A), while CIF keeps the minimum Clauses (C) cover. The parties can still agree otherwise.
  • FCA and on-board bills of lading. The parties can agree that the buyer instructs its carrier to issue an on-board bill of lading to the seller, which helps sellers who need one for a letter of credit.
  • Own means of transport. FCA, DAP, DPU and DDP now recognise that the buyer or seller may carry the goods with its own vehicles rather than hiring a carrier.
  • Clearer costs and security. Costs are listed in one place for each rule, and security-related requirements are allocated more explicitly.

Common mistakes and how to avoid them

Using FOB for containers

FOB was designed for cargo loaded directly over the ship’s rail. Containers are usually handed to the carrier at a container yard or terminal days before loading, so under FOB the seller carries risk for goods it no longer controls. FCA at the terminal or the seller’s warehouse matches how container shipping actually works. FOB remains extremely common in practice, so if you use it, understand the gap.

EXW when the buyer cannot clear exports

Under EXW the buyer is responsible for export clearance in the seller’s country, which a foreign buyer often cannot legally do. The seller ends up doing it informally, and the buyer may lack the export evidence it needs. FCA at the seller’s premises is usually the better choice.

DDP without understanding import taxes

Under DDP the seller must act as or appoint the importer of record, pay duty and often import VAT or GST, and may be unable to recover that tax. Some countries make it hard for a foreign company to import at all. Sellers should confirm they can do it legally before agreeing; buyers should check the price genuinely includes these costs.

Vague named places

“FOB China” or “DAP USA” invites disputes. Name a specific port, terminal or address, and refer to the version, such as Incoterms® 2020.

Frequently asked questions

Which Incoterm is best for a first-time importer?

Many small importers use FCA or FOB so they control the main freight through their own forwarder, or DAP if they prefer the supplier to manage transport. The right answer depends on your experience, the supplier’s capabilities and how much control you want over cost and timing.

Are Incoterms legally binding?

Incoterms rules apply only when they are incorporated into your contract, for example by writing the rule, the named place and “Incoterms® 2020”. Once incorporated, they form part of the agreement between buyer and seller.

Can I still use Incoterms 2010?

Yes, if both parties agree and the contract states the version. Incoterms 2020 is the current edition, so it is best practice to use it unless there is a specific reason not to.

Do Incoterms decide when I own the goods?

No. They deal with delivery, risk and costs. Transfer of ownership is governed by the sales contract and applicable law, so address it separately.

What is the difference between DAP and DDP?

Under both, the seller delivers to the named destination. Under DAP the buyer handles import clearance and pays duties and taxes; under DDP the seller does.
